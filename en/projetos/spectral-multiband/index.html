Spectral Multiband Decomposition: KV cache compression in LLMs

Independent research that treats an LLM's attention memory as a multispectral image and tests whether the query itself picks better which bands to keep. Ongoing research.

Problem

Language models spend GPU memory on the KV cache, and compressing that cache is an active research area. The hypothesis came from remote sensing: split the cache into bands, like a satellite image, and keep only the ones that matter for each query.

Data and scale

  • Three open models: Qwen2.5-1.5B, Qwen2.5-7B and Mistral-7B.
  • Evaluation on WikiText-2 (perplexity) and 5-shot MMLU.
  • Eight experiment sessions over five days, on a laptop GPU, with a research log.

Approach

  • Methodology plan with three research questions and falsifiable hypotheses, written before the experiments.
  • KV cache decomposed into 64 bands, keeping 16 to 64.
  • Static selector versus a selector guided by query-key alignment.
  • Six versions of the method; I also logged what did not work, such as PCA-learned bands.

How I validated it

  • Same battery for both selectors, at the same compression levels and on all three models.
  • Perplexity and MMLU measured together, so accuracy loss is not hidden behind a perplexity gain.

Result

  • On Qwen2.5-7B, the guided selector had lower perplexity than the static one at all 7 compression levels tested.
  • On Qwen2.5-1.5B and Mistral-7B, it won at 6 of 7.
