Mapping DATASUS: 991 GB of public health data, measured at the source

A survey, measured at the source, of Brazil's entire public health data archive, DATASUS (207,252 files, 991 GB), with the real data design, a map of diseases and the download cost of studying each one.

991 GBand 207,252 files inventoried; the core series fits in 12.8 GB

Where
GFX GEO
Period
2026
Role
Founder and engineer of record

Context

Anyone doing epidemiology with Brazilian public health data runs into an archive that is not a database: compressed files split by state and month, no patient key, and naming conventions full of traps. Nobody knows the download cost up front.

What I did

  • Crawled the whole public server and logged every file with its origin and size.
  • Documented the design of a database that does not exist: format, partitioning, version breaks, dictionaries and the lack of a patient key.
  • Mapped the 57 notifiable conditions and the full ICD-10, and showed that 13 of the 22 ICD chapters have no dedicated notifiable condition.
  • Built a catalog of 14,240 diseases with their sources, the basis for an API that answers what to download and how big it is.
  • Built an interactive dashboard on respiratory diseases, with a calculator for slicing by state and period.

How it works, in four stages

  1. Source

    • DATASUS public FTP
    • TabWin dictionaries

  2. Inventory

    • 207,252 files
    • Origin and size

  3. Catalog

    • ICD-10 and notifiable conditions
    • 14,240 diseases

  4. Output

    • Respiratory dashboard
    • API: what to download

Method

  • Everything measured at the source, nothing estimated.
  • Two access classes: diseases with their own file (dengue costs 1 GB) and diseases that exist only as a code inside hospital admissions and deaths (asthma costs 366 GB for the whole country).

Result

  • 207,252 files and 991 GB inventoried; the mortality, births and notifiable disease series fit in 12.8 GB.
  • 81% of the hospital admissions system is the same information republished in different formats.
