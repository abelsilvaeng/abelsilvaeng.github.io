Hydrological assessment of Lagoa Grande

An assessment of a town that floods, built on a 0.5 m terrain model and closed with a design flow for every point where water enters the lake.

Lagoa Grande, in Minas Gerais and the São Francisco river basin, grew around a lake of about 20 hectares and has a history of street flooding. The city government hired GFX GEO to find out where the water runs, where it pools and what to fix first. The drone survey covers roughly 540 hectares.

  • Designed and coded, in R and Python, the pipeline that goes from the terrain model to 15 thematic maps, fully parameterised and reproducible.
  • Replaced the satellite base used in the preliminary phase with the drone terrain model and merged the lake bathymetry into it.
  • Classified land cover on the orthophoto with Random Forest: roofs, pavement, bare soil, vegetation and water.
  • Delineated the urban catchments without filling depressions, treating the lake as a sink, which is how the town actually behaves.
  • Computed flow accumulation, HAND, RUSLE and a composite index to rank interventions.
  • Built the Rational Method spreadsheet per catchment, with the local IDF curve and documented alternative scenarios.
  • Terrain analysis on a 0.5 m DTM: D8, HAND, Strahler order, SPI and STI.
  • Random Forest on RGB bands, spectral indices and normalised height (nDSM).
  • RUSLE for erosion susceptibility.
  • Composite priority index with explicit weights, aggregated by catchment.
  • Rational Method with C = 0.80 and a 25-year return period.
  • 15 map products in 3D, a hydrology spreadsheet and vector data delivered.
  • 26 contributing catchments, 175 hectares of contributing urban area and 59.24 m³/s in combined design flow.
  • About 36% of the surveyed area is impervious (roofs and pavement).
  • Caveat stated in the delivery: one catchment was cut off at the edge of the flight and its flow is underestimated.

Water availability for urban supply

A repeatable method for the question every sanitation plan has to answer: can the source that supplies the town cope, and if not, where is the next water?

Small municipalities in the Alto Paranaíba region of Minas Gerais have no gauging station on the streams that supply them. Without local measurements, low flows have to be estimated from larger stations. I ran this study for the PMSB (municipal sanitation master plans) of five municipalities, as a member of the technical coordination team and the person responsible for the water availability studies for urban supply.

  • Delineated the catchment of every existing intake and of candidate catchments around the towns and districts.
  • Assembled the base from official sources: elevation model, stream network, water permits, boundaries and geology.
  • Estimated Q7,10 and Q95 for each catchment from five reference gauging stations.
  • Calculated the allocable flow from the regionalised Q7,10, net of the permits already granted upstream.
  • Classified each catchment as available or dependent on a reservoir and carried the result into the maps and the forward-looking volume of each plan.
  • Catchment delineation from flow direction and accumulation on the TOPODATA elevation model.
  • Regionalisation of low flows (Q7,10 and Q95) by drainage area.
  • Balance against permits already granted upstream of each point.
  • Thematic mapping with a verdict per catchment.
  • 30 catchments assessed across 5 municipalities, using 5 reference stations.
  • In 4 of the 5 towns, the catchment of the current intake has no allocable flow under Q7,10 and depends on a reservoir.
  • Alternatives with ample margin identified, the largest with 383.9 L/s allocable.
  • Method published in the Carmo do Paranaíba sanitation plan, available on the city government website.

Water quality background study for mining

A statistical study to separate, across 17 years of lab reports, what is naturally occurring from what may come from the operation.

A mining company answers for any parameter above the legal limit. Where the geology is rich in metals, part of those concentrations is natural. A background study shows what comes from the rock and can support site-specific reference values. I did this work for a multinational fertiliser company at a phosphate mining complex in Minas Gerais.

  • Led the study technically, as coordinating engineer of record, covering surface water and groundwater.
  • Brought 17 years of reports from different labs, sampling intervals and parameter lists into a single base by point and parameter.
  • Defined the statistical groupings: control points free of interference, the period before operations and later time windows.
  • Applied chronological scatter plots, boxplots and the median as the background estimator, with outlier detection.
  • Built the supporting map base: geology, hydrogeology, geomorphology, soils, land use and climate.
  • Conditional segregation of the dataset by spatial and temporal filters.
  • Control points selected through hierarchical analysis of spatial position.
  • Quartile boxplots, with the median as the background value.
  • Geological reading of the area to explain where concentrations come from.
  • A consolidated base of 38 monitoring points, 29 surface water and 9 groundwater.
  • Reference values proposed per grouping and compared with legal limits.
  • Final report issued in December 2021, in two revisions.

PIAT: crop yield forecasts below the municipality scale

A crop, climate and terrain data asset plus an in-house predictive model that estimates yield on cells of roughly 5.5 km instead of one number per municipality.

Available crop forecasts in Brazil work at municipality or state level, so every farmer in the same municipality gets the same number. I built PIAT at GFX GEO for Centelha 3 MG, a startup program run by FAPEMIG (the Minas Gerais state research foundation), with a pilot covering 25 municipalities in the Alto Paranaíba region: soybean, maize and sorghum, 2001 to 2023 harvests.

  • Built the data asset: crop production from IBGE/SIDRA (Brazil's statistics bureau), climate from FLDAS and CHIRPS, MODIS vegetation and temperature, ANADEM terrain and MapBiomas crop fractions.
  • Designed a cell grid at CHIRPS resolution with weak supervision: labels are municipal, features are per cell.
  • Ran more than 30 experiment versions, each one fully traceable.
  • Found and fixed a methodological leak: selecting features outside the fold was inflating sorghum R² by about 0.13.
  • Used a placebo test to reject an apparent gain from soil variables.
  • Wrote the submission package: scientific document, technical annex, pitch, timeline and 3D maps.
  • Leave-one-year-out temporal validation, with all preprocessing done inside the fold.
  • Placebo control: each candidate variable is tested against a shuffled copy of itself, across several seeds.
  • ExtraTrees with year as the technology trend; quantile-normal target transform for sorghum.
  • Shapley values (SHAP) for interpretation and conformal prediction for intervals.
  • Hydro-topographic proxies (TWI, slope) and crop-specific phenological windows.
  • R² on unseen years: 0.631 for soybean, 0.634 for maize and 0.490 for sorghum (Alto Paranaíba pilot).
  • Satellite-measured cropland fraction came out as the 2nd strongest driver for maize and the 3rd for soybean in SHAP.
  • Terrain does affect yield, but below the municipality scale: the municipal average washes it out.

GFX Hub: a public procurement platform

A GFX GEO SaaS product that collects, normalizes and searches public tenders from Brazil and other countries, with natural language search and alerts.

Anyone selling to government has to find the right tender before the deadline, and tenders are scattered across PNCP (Brazil's national public procurement portal), Sistema S, BNDES, state portals and multilateral banks. I designed and built the whole platform: collection, database, search, API, frontend and security.

  • Designed the multi-tenant architecture, with plans, quotas, API keys and JWT authentication.
  • Built incremental collectors with a watermark per source and tender type, rate control and resume on failure.
  • Worked out that the gaps in PNCP collection came from a windowed rate limit, not server errors, and fixed the collector.
  • Fixed deadline time zones: Brasília time was being stored as UTC.
  • Implemented full text search and a natural language parser that resolves the municipality by querying the database itself.
  • Extended collection to international sources such as the World Bank, IDB and the European Union, checking the license of each source.
  • Incremental collection with per-key watermarks and an adaptive window per tender type.
  • PostgreSQL search with tsvector, field weights, unaccent and trigrams.
  • Conversational search on Gemini, with automatic fallback to a backup model and a daily usage cap.
  • Two rounds of security audit: SSRF, zip bombs, account and IP lockout, LGPD-compliant deletion.
  • PNCP direct-purchase collection now runs with no errors after the rate limit fix.
  • Proposal deadlines shown at the correct time, with a countdown.
  • Frontend in the GFX GEO identity, with a cartogram of Brazil by state and a map by municipality.

Multi-criteria diagnostic tool

A municipality brings a shapefile, an elevation model and a drone flight; out comes the hydrological diagnosis with maps, a report and an urban cadastre detected by a neural network.

The tool grew out of the hydrological and urban drainage diagnosis I delivered through GFX GEO for Lagoa Grande, a town in Minas Gerais. The goal is to turn that hand-built study into a product: feed in a municipality's raw inputs and get the diagnosis automatically.

  • Designed the tool around one YAML file per municipality as the single contract, editable through a Streamlit interface.
  • Automated the hydrology: elevation model prep, urban catchment delineation, outlet detection, time of concentration and Rational Method spreadsheets.
  • Vectorized street centerlines in a cascade: curbs, segmentation and OpenStreetMap.
  • Trained a roof detector on weak labels and built the cadastral chain: walls, property, built area.
  • Generated the technical report from a template, with LLM-assisted writing and every number taken from the artifacts, never from the model.
  • Rational Method with time of concentration along the thalweg, over catchments delineated in GRASS GIS.
  • RUSLE and a priority index for the preliminary erosion diagnosis.
  • YOLO11-seg and object-based segmentation using the drone height model.
  • Spatial cross-validation by city block for the segment classifier.
  • Lagoa Grande: 26 catchments delineated and 15 thematic maps in the final delivery.
  • Roof detector at mAP@50 of 0.78 on a spatial split, above the acceptance threshold.
  • Segment classification at F1 of 0.953 under spatial cross-validation.

GeoBase: a geospatial database with a catalog

A GFX GEO PostGIS database that tells you, for any point or polygon, which datasets cover the area, at what scale and from what date.

Every water study used to start from scratch: downloading wells, water permits, geology, hydrography and boundaries. GeoBase came out of a hydrogeological study, but I designed it as a company asset, organized by theme with a spatial catalog on top.

  • Modeled the database in thematic schemas, with a spatial catalog you can query by geometry.
  • Wrote one idempotent load script per source and generated the documentation from the catalog itself.
  • Ingested public datasets: wells (SIAGAS, the national groundwater registry), water permits (IGAM, the Minas Gerais water agency), geology (CODEMGE), IBGE and ANA (the national water agency).
  • Implemented the first analysis: well water availability by radius, with geological context at the best scale available.
  • Spatial catalog that accepts points, polygons, shp, gpkg, kml and kmz.
  • Flow percentiles (P10, P50, P90) over growing radii, split by data type: pumping test, permit and registry.
  • Wells with pumping tests used as ground truth.
  • Database running locally, with the well availability analysis working.

Cartographic art

Public domain historical maps turned into wall pieces, with real terrain relief under the original lithograph.

Old maps can become art, but the usual route is manual GIS work, one piece at a time. I built a pipeline where georeferencing is automatic and the look is calibrated by measurement, not by eye. The piece shown here is the Crater Lake sheet (USGS, 1956), which is in the public domain.

  • Built the pipeline in stages, from sheet acquisition to final composition, with one config file per piece.
  • Georeferenced the sheet automatically by detecting the graticule and reading the coordinate labels with OCR.
  • Swapped the 30 m elevation model for USGS 3DEP at about 3 m, because 30 m blurred the caldera.
  • Split the sheet into ink layers and recomposed them over the relief.
  • Kept data and models with non-commercial licenses out of the pipeline.
  • Graticule detection and sequence-anchored OCR; projection fit and thin plate spline, validated on a holdout set.
  • 3D render in R/rayshader with the sheet draped over the elevation model.
  • Tone matching in Lab space against a measured reference.
  • Crater Lake 1956: holdout georeferencing error of 6.33 px, about 0.54 mm on paper and 33 m on the ground.
  • Tones matched to the reference by measurement: 5th percentile at 34.2 against 34.3, median at 79.1 against 79.2.
