PhD thesis: forecasting soybean harvests from satellite data alone

A machine learning model that estimates municipal soybean production in northern Mato Grosso using only satellite climate data, with no weather stations involved.

Crop forecasting in Brazil leans on weather stations, which are sparse and patchy right where the country grows most of its soybeans. My PhD asked whether satellites could do the job instead: 4 municipalities, harvests from 1989 to 2022, 24 climate variables from NASA's FLDAS/NOAH. The thesis is under embargo until 2027, so this is the method in broad strokes.

  • Automated the download of the full monthly FLDAS/NOAH series from NASA, with file integrity logging.
  • Processed the NetCDF files and pulled each climate variable for each municipality.
  • Turned 24 variables into 216 features per harvest, organized by month of the soybean cycle.
  • Joined the features with municipal production figures from IBGE/SIDRA and trained a gradient-boosted decision tree model.
  • Used SHAP values to see which variables matter, and in which month.
  • Took the method, together with my advisor, into the patent application filed by UFU.
  • Feature engineering by crop stage, built from satellite time series
  • Gradient-boosted decision trees with z-score standardization
  • Held-out validation with R², RMSE, MAE and residual analysis
  • SHAP interpretation and variable importance ranking
  • R² of 0.8232 on the validation set, for municipal production in tonnes
  • Water balance and radiation variables mattered most; the top one was groundwater runoff in October
  • Thesis defended and approved on 20 October 2025
  • Degree: PhD in Biofuel Science and Technology (UFU)

Master's: where canola can be grown

An agroclimatic zoning of canola for the Triângulo Mineiro and Alto Paranaíba region: where to plant it and how much irrigation it needs.

Canola is a winter oilseed used for biodiesel, and Brazil had few suitability studies for it. In my dissertation I combined climate and soil data for an entire region of Minas Gerais, using 2014 as the base year and automatic INMET weather stations in four states, to show where the crop fits.

  • Interpolated monthly mean temperature, rainfall and evapotranspiration from automatic weather stations.
  • Computed the water balance and the accumulated deficit month by month, with soil water retention of 30, 50 and 70 mm depending on texture.
  • Reclassified temperature, soil texture and water deficit with map algebra, following the criteria in MAPA Ordinance 326/2012 (MAPA is Brazil's agriculture ministry).
  • Produced the final zoning map, with five classes of suitability and irrigation need.
  • Spatial interpolation of station data
  • Monthly climatological water balance
  • Map algebra and reclassification in GIS
  • Soil type and texture base maps for the region
  • A five-class zoning, from suitable with occasional irrigation to restricted with mandatory irrigation
  • Monthly mean temperatures between 17 °C and 26 °C in the base year, with no area ruled out by temperature
  • Dissertation available in open access in the UFU repository

Spectral Multiband Decomposition: a satellite's view inside an LLM

An independent research project that treats an LLM's attention memory like a multispectral image and tests whether the query itself picks the bands worth keeping.

Language models spend GPU memory on the KV cache, and the best-known compression methods treat every input the same way. My hypothesis came from remote sensing: split the cache into bands, like a satellite image, and keep only the ones that matter for each query. This is ongoing research, not a finished technique.

  • Wrote the research plan with three research questions and falsifiable hypotheses.
  • Implemented six versions of the method and tested them on three open models: Qwen2.5-1.5B, Qwen2.5-7B and Mistral-7B.
  • Ran eight experiment sessions in five days on a laptop GPU, keeping a research log.
  • Recorded what did not work too, such as bands learned with PCA.
  • KV cache split into 64 bands, keeping between 16 and 64
  • Static selector compared with a selector guided by query-key alignment
  • Evaluation with WikiText-2 perplexity and 5-shot MMLU
  • On Qwen2.5-7B, the guided selector had lower perplexity than the static one at all 7 compression levels tested
  • On Qwen2.5-1.5B and Mistral-7B, it won at 6 of 7
  • The result is comparative: compression still costs MMLU accuracy, and real memory savings have not been measured
